# 1.2 Literature Review

This chapter reviews the literature and studies related to the development of a Web-Based Program Monitoring and Evaluation System for the Sangguniang Kabataan Federation. It provides background information and insights from previous research that support the design and implementation of the proposed system. The review is arranged by theme, covering digital governance in local government, youth governance and participation, centralized barangay information systems, records management and digital repositories, QR code technology for attendance and beyond, digital evaluation and feedback capture, notification and scheduling features, dashboards and analytics, monitoring and evaluation practice, inventory management, system development and quality evaluation, and access control and data privacy. Each theme closes with a synthesis of what the reviewed studies establish and what they leave unresolved.

---

## Digital Governance and Information Integration in Local Government

Government offices have moved steadily toward digital platforms, and research has shifted from asking whether an office has technology to asking whether the information it holds is joined together. A study using panel data from thirty-one provincial e-governments between 2015 and 2020 found that integrating separate government systems raises both the quality and the fairness of local public services, and that the improvement comes through better administrative efficiency and the spread of technology rather than from the tools themselves. The same study reported that the effect is strongest where fiscal transparency was previously low, which suggests integration matters most where records were hardest to examine [3].

Whether integration happens at all appears to depend on the organization more than on the technology. A structural equation modelling analysis of public sector organizations found that transformation moves forward where there is a sense of urgency, a recognized need for change, and a collaborative working environment, and that it stalls where organizational structures and managerial support are weak. That study also produced a finding that runs against expectation: resistance to change among staff was not among the factors that impeded transformation [4]. The barrier is structural rather than attitudinal.

Evidence from the Philippines shows what integration produces inside a single local government office. A mixed-methods evaluation of a Human Resource Information System in a Philippine city local government combined system usage metrics with interviews of staff and found reductions in payroll processing and onboarding times together with fewer data entry errors. Staff reported smoother workflows and better support for decisions, although usability problems and the need for further training remained. The study recommended continued training, ongoing system evaluation, and consideration of the model by other local governments [21]. At a broader scale, a systematic review of 122 studies published between 2000 and 2023 traced how performance management in local government shifted from New Public Management principles toward newer governance models after 2015, noting the growing place of technology in that shift and observing that public managers everywhere face the same difficulty of improving service quality with limited resources [22].

Conceptual work has distinguished electronic government, meaning the delivery of services through technology, from electronic governance, meaning the wider processes of decision-making and citizen involvement that technology supports [23]. A systematic literature review of electronic government adoption identified the key factors that determine whether citizens and offices take up such systems [24], while a meta-analytic structural equation modelling study examined how trust, risk, privacy, and security shape electronic government use [25]. Related work has examined how perceived government responsiveness moderates the path from citizen expectation and participation to satisfaction [26]. Digital transformation has also been examined at the level of a single application. A case study of a document management application in public administration found that efficiency gains from digital transformation in the public sector are often not measured and rarely examined over the long term, particularly outside Western Europe [27]. This is a methodological gap as much as a practical one, since offices that adopt systems seldom record what changed.

Taken together, these studies establish that joining separate systems produces measurable gains in efficiency and service quality, that the main barriers are organizational rather than a matter of user willingness, and that working with limited resources is a defining condition of local government. What none of them examines is a body operating below the municipal office. All of them study cities, provinces, or national agencies employing permanent staff with dedicated information technology support. An SK Federation has none of these. Its officers serve fixed three-year terms, hold no technical personnel, and coordinate across many barangays without direct authority over them. Whether the benefits documented at provincial scale appear at this scale has not been tested.

## Youth Governance and the Sangguniang Kabataan

The Sangguniang Kabataan Reform Act of 2015 restored and restructured the youth council system, assigning youth leaders the task of planning, implementing, and evaluating youth development programs. The law requires every SK to prepare a three-year Comprehensive Barangay Youth Development Plan and an Annual Barangay Youth Investment Program, pass resolutions, keep a youth database, and disclose its transactions to the public. It defines the Katipunan ng Kabataan as all residents aged fifteen to thirty and establishes the Pederasyon ng mga Sangguniang Kabataan at municipal, city, and provincial levels, with the federation president holding a seat in the corresponding local legislative body [1]. Amendments introduced in 2022 strengthened these provisions further [17]. Every duty the law assigns is a record-keeping duty before it is anything else, since a plan is a document, a database is a record, and disclosure requires that records exist and can be produced on demand.

Research conducted after the Reform Act suggests these duties are unevenly met. A national survey of youth leaders examined youth political participation and governance in the years following the law's ratification and found participation to be inconsistent, functioning more as a formality than as genuine democratic engagement [2]. Qualitative work supports this reading. Interviews and focus group discussions with SK officials across selected barangays in Baguio City found that councils treat youth profiling as the starting point for designing programs, yet continue to face inactive youth participation, limited budgets, and inadequate facilities and training [15]. Profiling is therefore recognized as foundational by the officials themselves, while the means to sustain it remain thin.

The most direct evidence comes from an assessment of SK performance in a single barangay measured against the specific mandates of the Reform Act. Among all indicators evaluated, two received the lowest ratings: the duty to conduct youth profiling and maintain an updated youth database, and the duty to make full public disclosure of transactions and documents [16]. Both are information management duties, and the study attributed the low scores not to unwillingness but to the absence of practical means of carrying them out.

Related work demonstrates why profiling matters for program design. A descriptive study of out-of-school youth across three barangays in Quezon City surveyed 194 respondents on demographic profiles, socioeconomic situations, educational attainment, and skill gaps, finding that forty-nine percent wished to return to school, twenty-one percent sought employment, and twelve percent aspired to entrepreneurship. The study argued that profiling provides a critical foundation for barangay officials addressing the needs of youth in their localities, while acknowledging limits arising from geographic scope and sample size [28]. Profiling, in other words, is not clerical work but the evidentiary base on which programs are designed.

The literature on SK governance converges on a consistent pattern. The law assigns duties that are documentary in nature. Officials themselves recognize records as the foundation of their work, and independent studies confirm that profiling data shapes what programs are offered. Yet the record-keeping duties are precisely the ones performed most poorly, and participation remains formal rather than substantive. This is a coherent picture of an institution whose obligations exceed its administrative tools. What is missing is any attempt at a remedy. These studies assess, survey, and describe, and the recommendations they offer address policy and training rather than systems. None builds or tests an artifact.

## Youth Digital Participation and Electronic Participation Platforms

Research on young people and technology has established that digital access shapes civic involvement. A study of individuals aged fifteen to thirty-five across more than thirty European countries, drawing on European Social Survey data, examined digital skills, digital citizenship, and civic engagement, identifying predictors and disparities among countries through chi-square tests and multiple regression analysis [29]. A study examining digital and socio-civic skills found these capabilities closely tied to how young people learn to participate and exercise citizenship in a digital age [30]. A three-wave longitudinal school-based survey conducted in six European countries, focusing on 2,660 adolescents aged twelve to seventeen who participated in all three waves, examined the interplay between youth vulnerability, digital skills, and civic engagement, and found a persistent divide in both access to and skilled use of digital resources among vulnerable young people [31].

Work on purpose-built participation platforms is more directly relevant. A qualitative study based on in-depth interviews with government officials examined a national online democracy service created to give young people under twenty-nine an equal opportunity to participate in political decision-making. The study identified challenges standing in the way of meaningful youth electronic participation, noting that whether the platform genuinely involved young people in decisions remained uncertain and required deeper investigation [32]. The finding separates two things easily confused: providing a digital channel and producing actual influence. A scoping review of interventions promoting youth civic engagement adds a further dimension, reporting that active participation in community improvement offers an opportunity to enhance young people's well-being and can act as a protective factor [33].

These studies establish that young people are digitally capable and that digital channels can widen access to civic life, while warning that a platform by itself does not guarantee meaningful participation. Their limitation, for present purposes, is one of direction. All of them look outward at how young people express civic identity or contribute opinions to government. None looks inward at how the youth councils representing young people manage their own operations. The internal information systems of small, volunteer-staffed youth governance bodies remain largely unexamined.

## Centralized Information Systems in Philippine Barangays

Philippine researchers have developed and evaluated a substantial number of barangay-level information systems, and this body of work forms the most directly comparable literature to the present study.

One study built a barangay information management system for a municipality in Nueva Ecija using the Scrum framework, moving through planning, requirements gathering conducted by barangay immersion, design and prototyping, development sprints, testing, deployment, and maintenance. Evaluated with seventy-five participants, it obtained an ISO/IEC 25010 grand mean of 4.62 alongside a Technology Acceptance Model mean of 4.74, with strong results for maintainability and security. The study identified fragmented data management as the condition the system was built to correct and emphasized modernizing record-keeping, educating barangay personnel, and applying stringent data practices [11]. A companion study of the same system, evaluated by fifty stakeholders comprising information technology professionals, barangay officials, and residents, reported an overall ISO/IEC 25010 mean of 4.44 with particularly strong compatibility and maintainability scores, and a Technology Acceptance Model grand mean of 4.34 led by perceived ease of use and behavioral intention to use. That study framed the problem as outdated manual processes, inefficient record-keeping, and fragmented data systems that delay essential services [34].

Another implemented a barangay information system on a web-based multi-tier architecture integrating a user-friendly interface, role-based business logic, a centralized database, and secure services. It reported a seventy-five percent reduction in document processing time and an eighty-eight percent improvement in data accuracy against a baseline in which document requests took two to three days, and it aimed to create a scalable model for community innovation [12]. A third applied Rapid Application Development to a resident information management system that simplified the storage and retrieval of records used for planning and document issuance [13].

More recent work has extended the pattern. A narrative review of public information systems, including a case study of barangay management information systems in the Philippines, examined objectives, best practices, and the challenges that recur in implementation [35]. A study developed a web-based information system for a barangay in Pasig City using the Waterfall model within the System Development Life Cycle, incorporating data analytics with a linear regression algorithm and evaluating the result under ISO/IEC 25010. The study observed that many barangays still rely on manual, paper-based systems for managing records and processing documents, producing delays, inaccuracies, and limited data accessibility that make timely, data-driven decisions difficult. It explicitly noted as a limitation that the system did not integrate with external or higher-level government systems such as municipal or national databases [36]. A study developed a barangay document requesting system for a barangay in Cabanatuan City, Nueva Ecija, using a modified waterfall model with a developmental research design involving information technology experts and residents, and evaluated its technical features through an ISO 25010 questionnaire that found both aspects generally acceptable while yielding recommendations for further improvement [37]. Domain-specific systems have also appeared, including a web-portal health information system developed for a barangay [38] and a combined mobile and web barangay management system delivering services across two platforms [39]. Another study assessed a web-based barangay management system for a rural barangay and observed that previous studies focused mainly on document requesting, resident profiling, or certificate issuance, leaving limited implementation and assessment of automated systems in smaller and rural barangays [40].

Work at the municipal level has begun to appear. A study developed a dynamic web-based information system for a municipality using open-source tools, with objectives of promoting transparency, showcasing municipal accomplishments, and providing access to essential services and financial data. User-end evaluation on a five-point Likert scale rated design and layout as very satisfactory, and a forum page enabled community feedback supporting continuous improvement. The study concluded that the implementation could be replicated in other municipalities [41]. A related study designed and evaluated an analytics-driven decision-support system for the Planning and Development Office of a local government unit in Nueva Ecija, responding to contextual planning and governance needs [42].

The closest existing work to the present study is a barangay web-based youth information management system with data analytics developed for a barangay in Pagadian City. That system replaced house-to-house profiling conducted on paper and the retyping of collected data into word processing documents. It combined data analytics, project monitoring tools, a dashboard for the SK Chairperson and Kagawad, and QR codes issued to authenticate SK clearances [14]. Its QR function verifies that a clearance document is genuine rather than recording who attended an activity, which is a different application of the same technology.

The most direct precedent is a system developed for the Local Youth Development Office of a municipality in South Cotabato. Built with Agile Scrum across planning, design, prototyping, testing, and implementation, with user testing among LYDO staff and SK officials, it integrates a centralized database and automated tracking tools for real-time SK project monitoring and youth profiling, and reports an overall evaluation mean of 4.63. Its stated problem is the one this study also addresses: manual data collection in barangays produces delays and inaccuracies that make project tracking and fund allocation difficult [43]. This work establishes that SK project monitoring at a level above the individual barangay is feasible and has been attempted.

Its scope, however, differs from the present study in ways that define what remains unaddressed. The system centers on project implementation monitoring and youth profiling for a local youth development office. It does not capture attendance at activities, does not collect evaluations from participants, holds no repository for minutes, resolutions, and other official documents, and does not track inventory of equipment and supplies. Nor does it give Katipunan ng Kabataan members their own accounts, since its users are LYDO staff and SK officials rather than the youth themselves. The present study therefore builds on this precedent rather than repeating it, extending federation-level monitoring to include participation capture, activity evaluation, document custody, inventory, and a member-facing account.

Three observations follow from this group of studies. The first concerns subject matter. These systems are built around residents, clearances, and certificates, and even the youth-focused system uses its QR feature for document authentication, so none produces the participation evidence the SK is legally required to generate. The second concerns scale. Almost all serve a single barangay, and where a municipal system exists it addresses transparency and financial disclosure rather than youth programs. None addresses the SK Federation, the municipal or city Pederasyon that oversees and consolidates the work of many barangay SK councils. The third is that the researchers themselves name the gap: one study explicitly lists non-integration with higher-level government systems as a limitation, which is precisely the consolidation problem a federation-level system must solve. Fragmented records impose the greatest cost where records from many barangays must be brought together, and that is the level at which no system has been built.

## Records Management and Digital Document Repositories

Official documents such as minutes, resolutions, letters, and reports require storage permitting reliable retrieval, and research on electronic records management addresses this need directly. A study of an integrated records information system used across government institutions, drawing on responses from one hundred government employees at various institutional levels, reported that respondents rated the system highly for accessibility, storage optimization, and collaboration between departments [5]. Work on electronic document management systems in electronic government environments has noted that the technology offers considerably more than the distribution, printing, and conventional file generation associated with paper-based practice [44].

Design-oriented studies describe what such systems contain. One study designed and implemented a web-based document management system, examining the components required for capturing, storing, and retrieving documents through a web platform [45]. A Philippine study developed a web-based document management system that digitized an existing paper-based document process, evaluating the result through stress testing and following IEEE standards and best practices for developing, documenting, and maintaining software projects [46]. A Philippine study developed a web-based records management system as an electronic records management initiative for an Office of Senior Citizen Affairs, demonstrating how digital tools strengthen organizational efficiency and improve the accessibility of public records within local government offices [47]. Research on decentralized document management has drawn attention to the security dimension, observing that document management systems can improve organizational efficiency, security, and compliance while remaining vulnerable to unauthorized access, document forgery, and malicious insider activity [48].

These studies establish that moving official documents into a single electronic repository improves retrieval, supports collaboration across offices, and reduces the labor of manual filing, while showing that access control is a design requirement rather than an optional feature. Their common setting, however, is the enterprise or the national agency, where documents follow standard workflows and dedicated staff maintain the system. The SK Federation produces a smaller and more varied body of documents maintained by officers whose terms expire, which raises a question of continuity these studies do not address: how records survive the turnover of the people who created them.

## Automated Attendance Capture and QR Code Technology

Manual attendance recording has well-documented weaknesses, and QR code systems have been repeatedly tested against them. One study built a web-based QR code attendance management system in which an administrator generates a code for each session, tracks scanned attendance, issues warnings to those with low attendance percentages, and produces customized reports used for calculating eligibility. The study reported reductions in paperwork, human error, wasted time, and forged signatures [6]. A study conducted in a Philippine secondary school surveyed one hundred twenty-four faculty, staff, and students on the implementation of a QR code-based attendance system, examining its accuracy gains against the difficulty of preserving face-to-face interaction between teachers and students [7]. A further study presented an online attendance monitoring system using QR codes within an academic setting [49].

Comparative work has assessed QR codes against competing technologies. Research on radio frequency identification developed an in-classroom faculty attendance monitoring system using ultra high frequency RFID with a captured image cross-verification mechanism, addressing the scarcity of studies monitoring attendance within the actual bounds of a classroom rather than at a campus entrance [50]. A more recent Philippine study developed an RFID-based attendance monitoring system with SMS notification and data analytics for senior high school and college students, integrating attendance capture with automatic messaging and reporting, and noting that earlier web-based applications for daily attendance could generate reports and evaluate attendance eligibility while conserving human and material resources [51].

Comparisons of the two technologies generally find that QR systems provide an effective inventory and identification backbone, offering fast identification with quicker and less expensive setup while still allowing reliable data collection and field updates [19]. The practical significance is that QR codes require no specialized reader, since any smartphone with a camera and a scanning application can read a code, which removes the equipment cost limiting RFID deployment in organizations without procurement budgets.

What this body of work establishes is that QR-based attendance capture reliably reduces paperwork and falsified records, produces attendance data in a form suitable for computing eligibility, and requires only equipment participants already carry. Two gaps remain relevant to the present study. Nearly all of these systems operate in schools, where attendance is taken repeatedly from a fixed and known roster in a fixed location, whereas SK activities draw varying participants to varying venues. More importantly, every reviewed system stops at recording presence. None continues from the attendance record into an evaluation of the activity just attended, which leaves the connection between participation and feedback unaddressed.

## Applications of QR Code Technology Beyond Attendance

QR code technology has diffused widely across sectors, and studies outside attendance contexts describe the underlying mechanism and its limits. QR codes act as a link between physical items or events and digital records, and their precise scanning, compact size, and high data storage capacity make them useful for attaching detailed information to each item [19]. A comprehensive review of QR codes in healthcare examined their integration into patient care, medication management, and health outcomes, documenting both applications and the perceptions of users [52]. A study of QR code strategies for promoting public health programs examined their use as communication tools, including wallet reference cards designed to improve access to program information [53].

Research in library and information services has documented adoption patterns. A study of QR code adoption in academic libraries drew on responses from thirty-seven systems librarians across one hundred four institutions, achieving a thirty-five and a half percent response rate, and found QR codes transforming administrative, academic, and communication processes while bridging physical and digital library resources [54]. A related study of QR code applications in academic libraries documented use across directional and promotional signage, collection access, information literacy teaching, and resource tracking, noting the sharp global growth in scanning volumes following the COVID-19 pandemic and the acceleration of contactless information sharing [55].

These studies establish that QR codes function reliably as a bridge between a physical or in-person context and a digital record, that they are now familiar to general users across many countries, and that their low cost and reliance on existing smartphones make them suitable for organizations without technical budgets. What they collectively demonstrate is the versatility of a single scan as an entry point to a digital process. This is the property the present study relies upon, since a code displayed at an SK activity serves not merely to confirm presence but to open the sequence that continues into evaluation.

## Digital Evaluation, Feedback, and Survey Instruments

Collecting evaluations of an activity raises questions of mode, timing, and data quality that have been studied extensively in survey methodology. A large-scale review of data quality evaluation practices analyzed 3,298 articles published in 2022 across two hundred journals, identifying eleven distinct assessment categories and finding that fifty-five percent of articles employed no data quality evaluation at all while twenty-four percent employed only one method. The most common indicators were attention-control items and nonresponse rates, and the study observed frequent use of strict and unjustified exclusion criteria [56]. The finding matters for system design because it shows that collecting responses is not the same as collecting usable responses.

Research on survey response rates has documented the shift in administration mode. An analysis of response rate trends noted that where only a small fraction of studies in earlier decades distributed surveys electronically, most survey data is now collected electronically rather than through paper-and-pencil methods, and proposed a validity assessment framework for evaluating response rates. The study argued that the goal is not always the highest possible response rate but the highest quality responses producing a sample appropriate to the study's purpose [57].

The comparison between electronic and paper instruments has been examined directly in field conditions. A participatory action research study conducted with fourteen data collection team members between November 2022 and October 2023 compared both methods and found that paper-based collection was time-consuming and produced errors in 12.8 percent of responses, amounting to 2,611 errors out of 20,398 responses, while the electronic tool once implemented was fast and simple with errors in 0.02 percent of responses. The study also documented why staff initially resisted the electronic method, citing fear of data loss from poor internet, insufficient competency due to lack of training, and unfamiliarity with technology [58]. A comparison of electronic and paper-based registers in a national health program similarly examined the completeness and concordance of records held in both systems [59]. In educational settings, related work has documented that a substantial portion of a target population may not respond to instruments administered outside a supervised setting, creating a risk of systematic sampling error [30].

Research on evaluating youth programs specifically has produced validated instruments. A study evaluating a curriculum-based positive youth development program applied a thirty-six item subjective outcome evaluation scale to 2,165 junior and 3,287 senior secondary students, measuring three dimensions: program quality, implementer quality, and perceived benefits. The scale showed satisfactory validity and reliability, students generally gave favorable evaluations, and senior students perceived the program more positively than junior students [60]. The three-dimensional structure is instructive for the present system, since an activity evaluation that captures only overall satisfaction discards the distinction between a poorly designed activity and a well-designed one delivered badly. Complementary work has examined the implementation of community-based youth participatory programs, identifying the barriers and facilitators that determine whether such programs are taken up and sustained [61], while a scoping review protocol has framed youth participatory evaluation as a model for monitoring outcomes that embraces participation and empowerment rather than treating young people solely as subjects of assessment [62].

Most directly relevant is a Philippine study that designed and implemented a web-based customer satisfaction survey system for a local government unit and evaluated it under ISO/IEC 25010, showing that citizen feedback collection can itself be delivered as a web system subject to standard software quality assessment [63]. A related study evaluated an existing institutional process as the basis for developing a web-based management system, establishing process evaluation as a design input rather than an afterthought [64].

This body of work establishes three things directly relevant to the proposed system. Electronic capture removes the encoding step that paper instruments require, since responses enter the database directly. Data quality depends on deliberate design rather than on mode alone. And the recognized weakness of electronic evaluation is response rate, which falls when respondents are asked to complete an instrument later and elsewhere. The design implication is specific: the reviewed literature suggests that an evaluation form presented at the moment of participation, in the same session as attendance, should avoid the response-rate penalty that afflicts evaluations distributed afterward. No reviewed study tests this arrangement, since the attendance literature and the evaluation literature have developed separately, and joining them is the particular contribution the present system attempts.

## Notification, Scheduling, and Reminder Features

Systems that manage activities must inform participants that activities are occurring. Research on reminders has produced quantified evidence of their effect. A systematic review and meta-analysis examining reminders for improving attendance analyzed ten studies covering 8,236 participants and found a statistically significant improvement in attendance compared with usual care. Subgroup analysis showed improvement associated with short message service reminders across four studies covering 4,636 participants and significant improvement associated with telephone reminders across five studies covering 3,369 participants, while noting that few studies reported delivery costs and none conducted a cost-effectiveness analysis [65].

Individual implementation studies support the finding. A study conducted at a hospital facility evaluated personalized reminder systems delivered by phone call or short message service according to patient preference, reminding 493 patients of appointments and comparing attendance rates before and after the intervention while accounting for demographic and clinical factors [66]. Another study analyzed non-attendance rates before and after the introduction of a short message service reminder system in a mental health service, using a mirror-image design comparing six months prior with six months following implementation [67]. Work in the Philippines has applied the same principle to institutional communication, developing a dual-platform alert system that delivers notifications across more than one channel to improve the likelihood that a message reaches its recipient [68]. Reminder research has also cautioned that simple automated messages are not equally effective for all recipients, since some populations may overlook them entirely.

These studies establish that automated reminders measurably improve attendance and that the effect is consistent enough to have been demonstrated through meta-analysis, while also showing that reminders are not uniformly effective across all recipients. Their setting is almost entirely clinical, where appointments are individually scheduled and non-attendance carries a direct resource cost. The application to youth programs is plausible but untested, and the reviewed literature offers no evidence on whether announcement and calendar features improve participation in voluntary community activities, where attendance is not an individual appointment and where the alternative to attending is simply doing something else.

## Activity Scheduling and Event Management Systems

Programs and activities must be scheduled, announced, and coordinated before they can be attended. Research on event management systems has examined how these functions are delivered through web platforms. A study designed and implemented a comprehensive college event management system covering the coordination of institutional activities [69], while another presented an event scheduler and coordination planner addressing the difficulty of aligning schedules across participants [70]. A further study developed a streamlined application for managing college events, focusing on reducing the administrative effort of organizing activities [71].

Philippine work in this area is directly comparable. A study developed an event management system for the technology innovation capstone program of a Philippine university, deploying it on cloud infrastructure with scheduled background jobs to automate recurring tasks [72]. Together these studies establish that activity scheduling, participant coordination, and announcement distribution are recognized system functions rather than incidental features, and that combining them into a single platform reduces the coordination effort that would otherwise fall on organizers.

What separates these systems from the present study is the population and the purpose. They serve institutions with enrolled members whose contact details are already held and whose participation is often expected or required. An SK Federation coordinates voluntary activities across many barangays for a membership defined by residence and age rather than enrollment, which makes the calendar and announcement functions a means of reaching people who are under no obligation to attend.

## Dashboards, Analytics, and Data-Driven Decision Making

Once records accumulate, they must be presented in a form supporting decisions. A scoping review of one hundred eight studies on public dashboards found that dashboards have become essential infrastructure for mediating public data and supporting decision-making in government, while noting that research in this area remains rooted in technical and quantitative traditions [8]. Philippine system studies have incorporated the same principle in practice, embedding real-time dashboards and analytics for administrative decision-making within developed systems [73], providing dashboards for the SK Chairperson and Kagawad within a barangay youth information system [14], and building analytics-driven decision support for a municipal planning office [42]. One barangay system incorporated a linear regression algorithm to support data-driven decisions by officials [36].

The value of a dashboard depends entirely on the quality of the records beneath it. This is the point at which the dashboard literature meets the integration literature reviewed earlier, since a dashboard drawing on scattered records can only display a partial view of operations. The reviewed studies assume that clean and centralized data already exists, examining what to do with it rather than how to obtain it. For an organization whose records are currently held in separate applications, that assumption is precisely what cannot be taken for granted, and the present study therefore treats centralization as a prerequisite for analytics rather than as a parallel objective.

## Monitoring and Evaluation Systems in Public Programs

Monitoring and evaluation is the function converting records into judgments about whether programs work. A study combining case studies with a practitioner survey established that the quality of a monitoring and evaluation system directly shapes evidence-based decision-making and improved development management [9]. Research on municipalities documented the conditions undermining this capability, identifying weak monitoring structures, inconsistent frameworks, and poor coordination between oversight institutions and local government as recurring obstacles [10].

These studies establish that monitoring and evaluation capability is not automatic and depends on structures, frameworks, and coordination rather than on intention alone. The literature, however, treats monitoring and evaluation as an administrative practice supported by frameworks and dedicated personnel, examining it in development agencies and municipal governments with evaluation staff. It does not address how a small volunteer body might carry out the same function through a system collecting evaluation data automatically at the point of participation. This is the specific approach the present study adopts, and it does not appear in the reviewed monitoring and evaluation literature.

## Inventory Management for Organizational Equipment and Supplies

Organizations holding equipment and supplies require a record of what they own and where it is. A study developed a web-based inventory management system for a small enterprise, enabling the owner to conduct regular inventory management through a web platform and addressing inaccuracies arising in the recording of incoming and outgoing goods [19]. A Philippine university study developed an online ICT equipment inventory and borrowing system with decision support, using a descriptive-developmental research design and Agile Scrum methodology. The system incorporated real-time inventory dashboards, digital borrowing workflows, automated notifications, and analytics for administrative decision-making, and was assessed under ISO/IEC 25010 with high compliance in functionality, reliability, and portability, alongside strong Technology Acceptance Model results for ease of use, usefulness, and satisfaction [73].

Proper inventory management allows an organization to follow what equipment is purchased during the period of procurement, assess the state of the equipment given out to employees, and understand what things should be replaced. For SK Barangay Officials, it will allow tracking of what equipment and supplies were used by youths during organizing activities in several barangays. However, the above inventory management systems can only be used by enterprises and universities with permanent property administrators [19], [73]. In the case of the SK Federation, the problem is how to organize inventory between shifts of the officials.

## System Development Approaches and Software Quality Evaluation

Philippine capstone and institutional research has converged on iterative development methods paired with standardized quality evaluation. Agile and Scrum approaches appear consistently, allowing stakeholders to review working increments and request refinements before the next cycle [11], [73]. A study developing an enterprise-level information system for digital governance in Philippine state universities and colleges applied Agile software methodology together with the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality model, demonstrating the pairing at institutional scale [74]. Other studies have applied the Waterfall model within a structured System Development Life Cycle where requirements were stable at the outset [36], and Rapid Application Development where rapid delivery was the priority [13].

The ISO/IEC 25010 standard has become the common instrument for assessing system quality. A bibliometric analysis of 1,613 articles drawn from Scopus between 1990 and 2025 traced the growth of research using this standard, showing a consistent rise in output peaking in 2023 and documenting application across public administration, finance, education, and healthcare [75]. Philippine studies routinely pair the standard with the Technology Acceptance Model, which predicts whether people will actually use a system by measuring perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use, and the two instruments are used together because they answer different questions. The standard assesses technical quality through expert judgment, while the acceptance model asks the intended users whether they would adopt what has been built, since a system can be sound in construction and still go unused [11], [34], [73]. This dual approach has become the expected standard for capstone-level system validation in Philippine research.

Related Philippine work demonstrates the same development pattern across different domains, including a web application for library resources with data visualization addressing limitations of traditional online public access catalogs [76], an interactive assistant for handling school inquiries [77], and a clinic medical record management system [78]. These studies collectively establish a methodological precedent: requirements gathered from actual stakeholders through immersion and interview, iterative construction, and dual evaluation combining a technical standard with a user acceptance measure. The present study adopts this same approach, placing it within an established local research practice rather than requiring a novel methodology.

## Access Control, Security, and Data Privacy

Any system collecting personal information operates under legal and technical constraint. Republic Act No. 10173, the Data Privacy Act of 2012, governs the handling of personal data by government entities and requires consent, security safeguards, and accountability in processing [20]. The requirement is not incidental to the present system, since youth profiles contain personal information and a portion of registered users will be minors.

Evidence on why this matters comes from research on citizen attitudes: a meta-analytic structural equation modelling study of electronic government use found that trust, risk, privacy, and security concerns together shape whether people are willing to use government systems at all [25]. Handling personal data properly is therefore not only a legal duty but a condition of adoption. Research on access control describes the mechanism by which this obligation is met technically. A study on implementing role-based access control strategies examined their application to mitigating insider risks across diverse organizational contexts [79]. Work integrating role-based access control with authorization protocols in a mobile application demonstrated how access management can be enforced systematically, adopting a structured development methodology to implement the control mechanisms [80]. An analysis of role-based access control in cloud security governance examined its place within broader security architecture [81]. Research on document management systems reinforces the necessity, showing that such systems remain vulnerable to unauthorized access, document forgery, and malicious insider activity unless access is deliberately controlled [48].

Role-based access control appears consistently across the reviewed Philippine systems as the mechanism limiting each user to the functions their position requires [12], [14]. Privacy compliance and access control are therefore the same design decision approached from two directions, one legal and one technical. What the reviewed literature does not address is the specific situation of an organization whose users include minors submitting their own information, which introduces consent requirements beyond those examined in the enterprise and institutional settings these studies cover.

---

## Summary

Based on the studies reviewed, researchers agree that centralized web-based systems make organizational work faster, more accurate, and better organized, that QR codes reliably capture attendance without paperwork or falsified signatures, that electronic forms remove the encoding step paper requires, and that dashboards, reminders, inventory tools, and Agile development paired with dual evaluation, one instrument for technical quality and one for user acceptance, is established practice in Philippine system research. The same studies show where the literature stops. Existing systems serve schools, businesses, and agencies with permanent staff; Philippine barangay systems center on residents, clearances, and certificates; attendance studies stop at recording presence without continuing into evaluation; and dashboard studies assume clean, centralized data already exists. Almost every one of these systems serves a single barangay, with one study naming non-integration with higher-level government systems as an explicit limitation, and where a system does reach above the barangay it covers project monitoring and youth profiling without capturing attendance, evaluation, documents, or inventory, and without giving Katipunan ng Kabataan members accounts of their own. Research on the Sangguniang Kabataan documents that youth profiling, database maintenance, and public disclosure are its weakest areas without building anything to fix them. Because of this, the researchers developed the Web-Based Program Monitoring and Evaluation System for the Sangguniang Kabataan Federation, which brings youth profiles, programs and activities, calendars, attendance, evaluation records, a digital repository, and inventory into one platform, uses a QR code displayed at an activity to open attendance and then the evaluation form for that same activity, provides dashboards, reports, notifications, and analytics for officials, and requires Katipunan ng Kabataan members to register for an account and log in before they can view the calendar, receive announcements, see accomplishments, and submit comments, all under role-based access and the Data Privacy Act of 2012, at the federation level existing systems have not reached.
